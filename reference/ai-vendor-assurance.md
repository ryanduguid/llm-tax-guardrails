# AI tool and vendor assurance checklist

Part of [DrDebits](../drdebits.md) `0.3.3`. Retrieve this file when a firm is assessing an AI product, vendor or in-house deployment before it reaches client work; verify it against `SHA256SUMS` in the release.


The AI-specific TPB rules in the guide require due diligence over confidentiality, privacy, security, access, retention, training use, subcontractors, location, incident response and exit arrangements. This checklist turns that requirement into questions a firm can send, with the evidence that answers each one, where the underlying obligation sits, and what follows when nobody answers.

The checklist names no vendor and favours none. Apply it to a hosted product, to a general-purpose assistant, to a firm’s own deployment, and to DrDebits and the runtime it loads into.

Three rules govern its use. An unanswered question is unanswered, not passed. Marketing copy, a certification badge, a partner tier and a security web page answer only the questions they address, and the firm records the rest as open. A completed checklist is a record of what the firm asked and what it received: it is not approval, it is not a compliance certification, and it leaves the practitioner’s accountability where it already sat.

The obligation column locates the operative text without quoting it. Retrieve the current source before relying on any row, and read the row as a DrDebits project control rather than as a statement of what the law or a standard requires.

## Authority and accountability

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-01 | Which actions can the product take without a person approving that specific action, and which require approval each time? | A written inventory separating read-only retrieval from every write, send, lodgement, payment and deletion, naming the approval step for each. | DrDebits Deployment, integrity and authority (state-change gate); Code Determination s 35 (competence and supervision); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (expected use and degree of reliance). | Treat every action as unapproved and keep the product away from any system that can write, send or lodge. |
| VA-02 | How does the product establish that the person approving an action holds authority for it? | Identity, role and engagement authority verified by the host system outside prompt text, with a record of who approved what. | DrDebits `AUTHORISED_HUMAN` definition; Code Determination s 40 (quality management); TASA Code item 7 (competent service). | The product is inferring authority from a login or from a claim typed into a chat, so its approvals carry no weight. |
| VA-03 | Which outputs does the product describe as reviewed, checked, approved or compliant, and what does a person actually do before that label appears? | Product copy, in-product labels and report footers read against the workflow that produces them. | TASA Code item 1 (honesty and integrity); APES 110 integrity `R111.1 to R111.3`; APESB Technical Alert 31 October 2025 (do not present a tool as reviewer or approver). | Unreviewed output may be reaching clients under a reviewed label. Change the label before the next file goes out. |
| VA-04 | Who carries a wrong figure the product produces, and what do the vendor contract and the practice indemnity policy say about it? | Liability, limitation and indemnity terms read alongside the current professional indemnity policy and any technology exclusion. | TASA Code item 13 (professional indemnity insurance); item 7 (competent service). | Assume the practitioner carries it, and confirm cover for the proposed use before client work reaches the product. |

## Confidentiality, privacy and data handling

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-05 | Which organisations receive client information when the product runs, counting model providers, hosting, support and subprocessors? | A current subprocessor list naming each recipient, its role and its location, with a commitment to notify changes. | TASA Code item 6 (confidentiality); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (subcontractors, location); APES 110 `R114.1`, `R114.2`, `AUST R114.3`, `R114.4`; Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. | No client can give informed permission, because nobody can say who receives their information. |
| VA-06 | In which countries is client information processed and stored, counting backups, logs and support access? | Named regions per data store, plus the locations from which support staff can view client content. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (storage location, access); Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; TASA Code item 6. | Record the location as unknown. Cross-border disclosure cannot be assessed, so it cannot be permitted. |
| VA-07 | Is client information, prompt text or output used to train, fine-tune, evaluate, benchmark or improve any model, by the vendor or by a subprocessor? | A contract term covering the vendor and every subprocessor, rather than a settings toggle or a sentence on a web page. | TASA Code item 6 (confidentiality); APES 110 confidentiality across the complete data lifecycle; TPB(GS) 55/2026 (training use). | Treat everything sent to the product as disclosed for a purpose the firm has not agreed to. |
| VA-08 | How does the product keep TFNs and other identifiers the task does not need out of prompts, logs, caches and exports? | Field-level handling documented at each of those points, with the redaction step named and observed on a test file. | Privacy (Tax File Number) Rule 2015; DrDebits Data and privacy gate; the non-negotiable stop on exposing a TFN. | Assume TFNs reach the logs, and keep them out of the product until the handling is shown. |
| VA-09 | How long does client information persist in prompts, logs, caches, embeddings and backups, and how is deletion evidenced? | A retention period per store and a deletion record the firm can produce later, covering derived stores as well as uploaded files. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (retention); Code Determination s 30 (records the practitioner must keep for at least five years); the Australian Privacy Principles (information the firm must not hold longer than it needs). | The firm can neither prove deletion nor produce the record it is required to hold. |
| VA-10 | Can one client’s information be identified and exported, and can the part of it the firm is not required to keep be destroyed, including from derived stores? | A demonstrated single-client export and deletion on a test tenancy, with the records the practitioner must retain identified and held back from the deletion. | Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, read with the records Australian law requires the firm to keep; Code Determination s 30 (records retained for at least five years); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (exit arrangements); TASA Code item 6. Retrieve both sides before acting: neither destruction nor retention is unconditional. | The firm can neither answer a client request nor separate the records it must retain from the data it should not hold. |
| VA-11 | What must the firm tell a client before their information is entered, and does the vendor support that disclosure? | Wording naming the recipient, the processing and storage locations and the AI use, in terms the client can act on. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (client permission unless a legal duty to disclose applies, with the recipient, location and AI use explained); TASA Code item 6. | Permission obtained without those facts is not informed permission, so hold the client information back. |

## Grounding, sources and calculation

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-12 | Where do the rates, thresholds, dates and labels in the output come from, and who updates them? | A named source per figure with a check date and a documented update process, rather than a model recollection. | TASA Code items 8 and 10 (current knowledge and skills; correct application of the tax law); DrDebits Source status and authority. | Treat every figure as unverified and check it at the primary source before use. |
| VA-13 | Which monetary and statutory figures does the product compute in code, and which does the model generate? | A field-by-field statement, plus a sample the firm reperforms independently. | DrDebits Mandatory workflow (deterministic computation preferred over generated arithmetic); APES 110 reliance on technology output `R220.8`, `220.8 A1`; `R320.11`, `320.11 A1`. | Reperform every figure. Fluent presentation is not evidence of arithmetic. |
| VA-14 | What does the product do when a material fact is missing or a source cannot be verified? | Two runs the firm performs itself: one file with a missing fact, one with an unreachable source. A product that answers anyway is the finding. | DrDebits decision statuses `NEEDS_FACTS` and `ESCALATE`; the `SOURCE CURRENCY NOT CONFIRMED` label. | Assume the product fills the gap and presents the result as settled. |
| VA-15 | Can a reviewer see the sources behind one specific figure, at the point of that figure? | A citation per material assertion that resolves to a retrievable primary source, checked on a live file rather than a demonstration. | DrDebits Mandatory workflow (primary-source grounding); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (verify AI content throughout the workflow). | The output cannot be verified, so it cannot support a client deliverable. |
| VA-16 | How does the product treat instructions that appear inside client documents, emails and tool responses? | A documented trust boundary and an observed run on a document carrying an embedded instruction. | DrDebits Trust boundary and instruction integrity; behaviour tests INJ-001 and INJ-002. | Assume a client document can redirect the product, and give it no tools that act. |

## Human review and supervision

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-17 | Which review step is mandatory before output reaches a client, and can a user or an administrator switch it off? | The configuration that enforces it and the record of who reviewed each item. | Code Determination s 35 (competence and supervision); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (AI output assessed and supplemented by professional judgement). | Treat the review step as optional in practice and impose the firm’s own control instead. |
| VA-18 | What does a reviewer see: the output alone, or the inputs, sources, assumptions and every change the product made? | A worked example of the review view on a file of realistic size and untidiness. | APES 110 competence, due care and supervision `R113.1 to R113.3`; the conceptual framework requirement to document significant judgements. | The reviewer is approving a conclusion they have no means to test. |
| VA-19 | How does the product report what it could not resolve, rather than resolving it? | An exceptions or client-query list produced on a test file, with the unresolved items visible to the reviewer. | DrDebits Output contract; APES 110 integrity (disclose relevant limitations). | Silence is being presented as completeness. |
| VA-20 | What capacity or turnaround does the vendor claim, and how much review time per item does that claim assume? | The claim, the review time it assumes, and the firm’s measured review time on a sample of its own files. | APES 110 objectivity and undue influence `R112.1 to R112.2`; inducements and pressure sections 250, 270 and 340; APESB Technical Alert 31 October 2025 (avoid undue reliance on technology). | A capacity figure that leaves no room for review is a proposal to shorten review. Cost the review time before the firm commits to the figure. |

## Records, evidence and disclosure

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-21 | What record does the product produce of AI use on a file: inputs, sources, checks, changes and the reviewer? | The record it emits on a test file, in a form the firm’s quality-management system can hold. | Code Determination s 30 (proper client records); DrDebits Workpaper record; TPB(GS) 55/2026 (document the review and any challenged or changed output). | The firm cannot show a reviewer or a regulator what the model contributed. |
| VA-22 | Can the firm reproduce an output from six months ago, with the model version and the sources that produced it? | Version pinning, retained inputs and a reproduction run on an archived file. | Code Determination s 30 (records retained for at least five years); s 40 (quality management). | Last year’s workpaper cannot be defended this year. |
| VA-23 | How is AI use disclosed to the client, and who controls the wording? | The disclosure text, where it appears in the deliverable, and the firm’s ability to change it. | APESB Technical Alert 31 October 2025 (disclose AI use, supervise and review); TPB(GS) 55/2026 (explain the recipient, the processing or storage location and the AI use as appropriate); law, engagement terms or a service-specific APES standard where disclosure is mandatory. | The firm is disclosing on the vendor’s terms, or not at all. |

## Security, access and change

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-24 | Who at the vendor can read client information, under what approval, and where is that access logged? | The access model, the approval path for support access, and a log the firm can request for a named period. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (access, security); Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles; TASA Code item 6. | Assume support staff can open any file the firm uploads. |
| VA-25 | What independent assurance covers this product, what period does it cover, and what did the assessor leave out of scope? | The report or attestation with its scope section, obtained under a confidentiality agreement where needed. A partner tier, a certification badge and a security web page are not the report. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (due diligence over confidentiality, security and access); Code Determination s 40 (quality management). | Record the product as carrying no independent assurance. A partner badge attests to a commercial relationship, not to how this product handles client data. |
| VA-26 | How is the firm told when the model, the prompts or the product behaviour change? | A change-notice channel, a version identifier visible in the product, and the notices issued over the last twelve months. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (understand the tool’s capabilities and limitations); APES 110 conceptual framework (re-evaluate when tool behaviour changes). | The product the firm tested is not the product it is running today. |
| VA-27 | Can the firm test a change before it reaches client work? | A staging environment or an opt-in window, with the firm’s own regression cases run against it. | Code Determination s 40 (quality management); APES 110 competence, due care and supervision `R113.1 to R113.3`. | Every change lands on live client files first, and the firm finds out from the output. |

## Continuity, incidents and exit

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-28 | What happens when client information is exposed, and how quickly is the firm told? | The notification commitment in hours, the contact path, and what the vendor supplies for the firm’s own assessment. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (incident response); the breach-notification obligations in the Privacy Act 1988, retrieved at their current text; DrDebits Data and privacy gate. | The firm hears about its own breach from the client, or not at all. |
| VA-29 | If the vendor fails, is acquired or retires the product, what happens to client information and to work in progress? | Exit terms covering export format, assistance period, deletion certification and the obligations that survive termination. | TPB(GS) 55/2026 (exit arrangements); Code Determination s 30 (records the practitioner must still hold). | The firm’s client records depend on the vendor staying solvent and interested. |
| VA-30 | What work stops if the product is unavailable during a lodgement period, and what is the fallback? | A fallback the firm has run once at deadline volume, not a described one. | TASA Code item 7 (competent service); item 12 (client rights, obligations, deadlines and consequences within the engagement scope). | The lodgement timetable now depends on the vendor’s uptime. |

## Conflicts, independence and inducements

| ID | Question | Evidence that answers it | Where the obligation sits | If nobody answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VA-31 | Do the vendor, its investors or its partners hold a relationship with a client, a referral source or a competitor of the firm? | Ownership, investor and partnership disclosures, checked against the client and referral lists. | TASA Code item 5 (conflicts); APES 110 conflicts sections 210 and 310. | A conflict nobody has identified cannot be managed or disclosed. |
| VA-32 | On an assurance engagement, does the product decide anything, or does it only propose? | The specific outputs it produces on that engagement, referred to the engagement partner before any use. | APES 110 Parts 4A and 4B; DrDebits Independence and assurance; behaviour tests IND-001 and ACT-001. | Keep the product off that engagement until the engagement partner has decided. |
| VA-33 | What commercial incentives attach to volume, to referrals or to the firm promoting the product? | Fee structure, rebates, referral terms and any marketing obligation in the contract. | APES 110 inducements and pressure sections 250, 270 and 340; TASA Code item 4 (the client’s lawful best interests). | An incentive the firm has not written down is one it cannot weigh against the client’s interests. |
